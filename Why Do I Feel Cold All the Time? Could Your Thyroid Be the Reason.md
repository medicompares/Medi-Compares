<p>Feeling cold once in a while is completely normal, especially during winter or when you're sitting in an air-conditioned room. But if you <strong>feel cold most of the time while other people around you feel comfortable</strong>, it may be worth paying attention to. One possible reason is an <strong>underactive thyroid, also called hypothyroidism</strong>. Your thyroid produces hormones that help your body use energy and regulate several important functions. When thyroid hormone levels are too low, your body's processes can slow down, which may make you feel unusually cold.</p>

  <p>Feeling cold by itself does not mean that you have a thyroid problem. There can be many other reasons, such as poor sleep, stress, low body weight, nutritional problems, certain medicines, anemia, or simply being in a cold environment. However, if feeling cold continues for a long time and you also notice <strong>constant tiredness, unexplained weight gain, dry skin, hair changes, constipation, low mood, or difficulty concentrating</strong>, it may be a good idea to speak with a healthcare professional.</p>

  <p>If your thyroid is underactive, you may notice that your energy levels are lower than usual. You might feel tired even after sleeping well, find everyday activities more difficult, or feel like your body is moving more slowly than before. These changes can happen gradually, so many people don't immediately realize that their symptoms could be connected to their thyroid.</p>

  <p>An underactive thyroid can also be associated with <strong>unexpected weight gain</strong>. This doesn't necessarily mean that you are eating more than before. Thyroid hormones are involved in how the body uses energy, so low hormone levels can contribute to changes in body weight. However, weight gain has many possible causes, which is why it shouldn't automatically be blamed on the thyroid.</p>

  <p>Another change you may notice is <strong>dry skin or hair changes</strong>. Some people with hypothyroidism experience skin that feels drier than usual or changes in their hair. Constipation, low mood, muscle weakness, and difficulty concentrating can also occur. Having one symptom doesn't confirm a thyroid problem, but several symptoms appearing together may be worth discussing with a doctor.</p>

  <p>The good news is that hypothyroidism can be evaluated with <strong>thyroid blood tests</strong>. A healthcare professional can look at your symptoms, medical history, and test results to determine whether your thyroid may be underactive. Getting the right diagnosis is important because many conditions can cause symptoms similar to hypothyroidism.</p>

  <p>If your doctor confirms hypothyroidism, treatment may include <strong>thyroid hormone replacement medicine</strong>, such as levothyroxine. <strong>Thyronorm 75mcg Tablet</strong> contains thyroxine and may be prescribed when thyroid hormone replacement is needed. However, 75mcg is not the correct dose for everyone. Your doctor decides the appropriate strength based on your thyroid test results and individual health needs.</p>

  <p>If you are prescribed Thyronorm, taking it consistently is important. Your doctor or pharmacist may provide instructions about taking it on an empty stomach and keeping appropriate timing between the medicine and certain foods, supplements, or other medicines. Calcium and iron supplements, for example, can affect thyroid hormone absorption when taken too close to the medicine.</p>

  <p>It is also important to remember that <strong>Thyronorm should not be taken simply because you feel cold or want to lose weight</strong>. Thyroid hormone replacement is intended for people who need it. Taking more thyroid hormone than prescribed can cause unwanted effects, including symptoms related to having too much thyroid hormone in the body.</p>

But symptoms alone cannot confirm hypothyroidism. The best approach is to speak with a healthcare professional and get appropriate testing if recommended.</p>
